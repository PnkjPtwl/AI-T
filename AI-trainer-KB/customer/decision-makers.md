# Phoenix Automotive - Key Decision Makers

## Overview

The ongoing engagement between Phoenix Automotive and Relanto involves multiple stakeholders across procurement, manufacturing, and executive leadership. While technical evaluations are being led by the manufacturing team, final commercial approval requires alignment between operational and executive decision makers. Understanding each stakeholder's responsibilities and priorities is essential for progressing both active opportunities.

## Sarah Thompson

Sarah Thompson serves as the Project Manager for Phoenix's Manufacturing Modernization Program and is the primary point of contact for the current engagement with Relanto. She is responsible for coordinating technical evaluations, scheduling workshops, collecting feedback from internal engineering teams, and ensuring that proposed solutions align with project objectives.

Sarah is particularly interested in improving manufacturing quality, reducing manual inspection effort, and introducing AI-driven technologies that provide measurable operational improvements. During discussions, she focuses on implementation timelines, system integration, product reliability, and long-term support rather than commercial negotiations.

Although Sarah does not make the final purchasing decision, her technical recommendation carries significant influence during the vendor evaluation process. A successful proof of concept and positive implementation plan are critical for gaining her approval.

## Michael Brown

Michael Brown is the Vice President of Operations and acts as the executive sponsor for Phoenix's manufacturing transformation initiatives. He is responsible for approving strategic investments, reviewing commercial proposals, and ensuring that new supplier partnerships align with the company's long-term business objectives.

Michael primarily evaluates projects based on return on investment, implementation risk, supplier stability, and long-term partnership value. While he relies on recommendations from the technical and procurement teams, final commercial approval requires his confidence that the proposed solution will deliver measurable business outcomes.

He becomes actively involved during the later stages of the sales cycle, particularly after technical validation and commercial negotiations have been completed.

## Decision Process

The evaluation process begins with technical validation led by Sarah Thompson and the engineering team. Once the proposed solution satisfies the required technical and operational criteria, commercial discussions are reviewed internally before being presented to executive leadership.

At the current stage of the engagement, both active opportunities have successfully completed the discovery, technical evaluation, and product demonstration phases. The Brake System Modernization opportunity is progressing through commercial negotiations, while the AI Quality Inspection Platform remains under technical evaluation. Final approval for either opportunity will require executive review and agreement on commercial terms.
